Get Evidence in an Afternoon

How to turn a preference into a level-2 reason on a physical product, without a budget. Measured or observed, not opined — and stated at the size it was seen.

From Lesson 03 Use when Your reasons are all level 4–5 Pairs with The Justification Ladder

Where to look, cheapest first

SourceCostWhat to do
Already ownedHours · freeReturns and warranty codes, service tickets, dealer notes, supplier failure data, test-lab reports. Ask for a specific slice, not “the data”.
Measure itAn hour or twoForce gauge, scale, calipers, tape — on yours and the comparison product. At least three units, so you see the spread.
Watch hands on the real thingOne afternoonSix people, the current product, one task, real conditions. The failure you're claiming already lives in it.
Test a stand-inDaysFoam, print, taped mock. For size, reach, grip, force — not finish or feel.

The four-line plan

1 · Claimcould be false
What the part does or people can do, not what they'd like. “Riders in winter gloves can open the new latch unaided.”
2 · Testwatch, don't ask
Measure it, or one action in real conditions by people who resemble the user and aren't in the studio. Say nothing; observe. Name who runs it.
3 · Linebefore you look
The result that changes your mind. “Two or more of six fail and we don't ship it.” Written down first.
4 · Sentenceright-sized
Count, group size, condition — and what it doesn't show.

Template

“In a test of [n] [units or people], [doing what], [k] [what happened]. That shows [it happens / it holds]. It doesn't show [how often / how long].”

What a small test can and can't say

You sawCan sayCan't say
Some failures in 3–6This problem exists. This is what it looks like. This is what to fix.How many riders it affects. Any percentage.
No failures in 6A good sign.That it's fine: at 95% confidence the true failure rate could still be about 39%.
No failures in 10 / 30Better — still not a sign-off.True failure rate could still be about 26% / 10%.
No failures in 22 / 59At least 90% reliable at 90% confidence / 95% at 95%. Lab work.Anything at all, if even one piece failed — the formula no longer applies.

Quick rule: zero failures in n trials → failure rate could still be up to about 3 ÷ n (good above roughly 30). Success run: n = ln(1 − confidence) ÷ ln(reliability). Small and repeated beats big and once: test, fix what you saw, test again.

Failure modes

Asking what they prefer — six level-5 opinions, not evidence. Drawing the line afterwards — every result agrees with you. Testing colleagues — they share your eye. Quoting a percentage from six people — the over-claim, in a lab coat. “Six for six, so it's fine” — six clean passes are a good sign, not a guarantee. A test that can't fail — a demonstration, not evidence.
