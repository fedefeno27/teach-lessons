Get Evidence in an Afternoon

How to turn a preference into a level-2 reason without a budget. Observed, not opined — and stated at the size it was seen.

From Lesson 03 Use when Your reasons are all level 4–5 Pairs with The Justification Ladder

Where to look, cheapest first

SourceCostWhat to do
Already ownedHours · freeReturns and warranty codes, service tickets, dealer notes, old test reports. Ask for a specific slice, not “the data”.
Test what existsOne afternoonSix people, the current product, one task. The failure you're claiming already lives in it.
Test a stand-inDaysFoam, print, taped mock. For size, reach, grip, force — not finish or feel.

The four-line plan

1 · Claimcould be false
What people can do, not what they'd like. “Riders in winter gloves can open the new latch unaided.”
2 · Taskwatch, don't ask
One action, in real conditions, people who resemble the user and aren't in the studio. Say nothing; observe.
3 · Linebefore you look
The result that changes your mind. “Two or more of six fail and we don't ship it.” Written down first.
4 · Sentenceright-sized
Count, group size, condition — and what it doesn't show.

Template

“In a test with [n] [who], [doing what], [k] [what happened]. That shows [it happens]. It doesn't show [how often].”

What a small test can and can't say

Group sizeCan sayCan't say
3–6 peopleThis problem exists. This is what it looks like. This is what to fix.How many riders it affects. Any percentage.
20+ peopleStatistically meaningful numbers — and even then, wider margins than you'd like.Why. Watching is still what tells you why.

Small and repeated beats big and once. Three rounds of five: test, fix what you saw, test again.

Failure modes

Asking what they prefer — six level-5 opinions, not evidence. Drawing the line afterwards — every result agrees with you. Testing colleagues — they share your eye. Quoting a percentage from six people — the over-claim, in a lab coat. A test that can't fail — a demonstration, not evidence.
