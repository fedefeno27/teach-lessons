The Justification Ladder

How hard you are allowed to push depends on what your reason rests on. Rung 1 ends arguments. Rung 5 starts them.

From Lesson 02 Use when Someone asks why it's like that Pairs with The Decision Sentence

The five rungs

1

Physics & law

Material limits, process constraints, safety standards, regulation. It cannot be otherwise.

Unarguable
2

Evidence

Field failures, warranty data, ergonomic measurement, user testing. We tried it and this happened.

Very strong
3

Money

Commercial consequence with its volume attached. See The Decision Sentence.

Strong
4

Strategy

Range coherence, brand, roadmap. Legitimate, and someone may hold a different strategy in good faith.

Contestable
5

Judgement

My trained eye. Often the truest reason you have and always the weakest one in the room.

Costs capital

The two rules

Climb down, never up. Open at the highest rung you honestly stand on, then descend. Never decorate a rung-1 argument with taste — it drags the whole thing to rung 5.
Label rung 5 out loud. "That's my judgement, not a constraint." Saying it costs you the odd argument and buys you being believed when something genuinely cannot move.

When challenged: four beats

1 · Pausesay nothing
Let them finish. Leave a beat after they do.
2 · Probeone question
"What's the concern underneath that?" · "If we changed it, what would that fix for you?" Then stop talking.
3 · Placesilently
Which rung does the answer to their real concern stand on? Take the highest honest one.
4 · Answerthe three questions
What problem does it solve? How does it affect the user? Why is it better than the alternative?

Content of the answer

Greever's three questions cover everything a stakeholder needs. The third is where most answers fail, because it requires an alternative you can describe fairly.

QuestionWhat it forces you to have
What problem does it solve?A problem stated in their terms, not a feature stated in yours.
How does it affect the user?A consequence for a real person, not an attribute of the object.
Why is it better than the alternative?An alternative, held in mind, describable without contempt.

Failure modes

The over-claim — a rung-5 preference dressed as a rung-1 constraint. Survives until one person demonstrates a version that works; costs you every future justification.
The under-claim — filing a hard constraint as taste. Hands away an argument you had already won.
Answering unprobed — a competent reply to an objection nobody made.
Defending everything equally — reads as precious, and loses you the two decisions that mattered.

If your reasons are all rung 4–5

The decision is weaker than your conviction about it. Go and get a rung-2 reason — an afternoon of testing with six people converts a preference into evidence, and no amount of arguing does.
