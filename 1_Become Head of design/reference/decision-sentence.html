The Decision Sentence

Say this before you show anything. Print it, keep it in the notebook you take to reviews.

From Lesson 01 — Lead With the Number Use before Any review where you need a yes

The four slots

1 · Outcomethe number
Business result, in a metric they already track, with the volume or period attached. £4 a unit at 40k. Six weeks earlier. Half the warranty claims.
2 · Mechanismthe design move
What you'd change, in one clause. Craft appears here as the cause of the number — never as the argument itself.
3 · Costthe downside
Money, time, or risk — named by you first. Volunteering it turns a pitch into a recommendation. Withhold it and your caveat returns as their objection.
4 · Askthe decision
Exactly what you need decided, by when, and why that date is real. No ask means it was a status update.

Template

“[Mechanism] gets us [outcome] at [volume]. It costs [cost]. I need [decision] by [date] because [why the date is real].”

Four checks before you speak

The arithmetic

gross margin = (net sales − COGS) ÷ net sales × 100
TermMeansWatch for
COGS Direct cost of production: raw materials, direct labour, manufacturing overhead. Excludes admin salaries and R&D. On physical product, BOM + tooling + supplier choice set most of it — and design sets much of those.
Gross profit A figure in pounds. Sales minus COGS. Not comparable between products of different sizes.
Gross margin A percentage of revenue. What is left after COGS. Confusing this with gross profit is the tell of a visitor to the commercial conversation.

Benchmarks. Software 70–90%. Manufacturing and retail 20–50%. In a 35% business, £1 off unit cost is worth roughly £3 of extra revenue — the most portable fact on this page.

The three questions in the room

They askThey sayYou control
Does this make or cost money?Margin, price point, volumeMaterial, part count, finish, assembly steps
Does this make us late?Tooling slot, launch windowHow reversible the change still is
Does this expose us?Warranty, returns, compliance, brandDurability, safety, coherence with the range

Failure modes

Leading with effort — "we ran three studies" describes the only cost nobody is deciding about. Leading with taste — true, unactionable, goes on slide three. Unquantified user sentiment — taste wearing the clothes of research. No ask — nothing gets decided, and you will be asked to come back.
