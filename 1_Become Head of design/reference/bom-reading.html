Reading a BOM

Find where a design decision lands in the bill of materials — and say what it costs in the finance lead's terms.

From Lesson 05 Use when Someone says “that will cost more” Pairs with The Decision Sentence

Where the cost sits

WhereMeaningYour lever
Standard partsOff the shelfStandard over special; fastener count
Custom partsMade to your drawingMaterial, process, finish, tolerance, tool
AssemblyLabour and timePart count, fixings, steps
OverheadIndirect support costsMostly not yours — complexity feeds it

Fixed vs variable. Tools are paid once; parts are paid per unit. One-off ÷ volume = tooling per unit. Keep them apart.

Three passes

1 · Countcomplexity
Lines, distinct parts, fasteners, suppliers.
2 · Rankwhere to argue
Sort by cost. What share do the top five lines hold? Check, don't assume.
3 · Questioneach top line
Standard or custom? What process? Fixed or variable? Which decision of mine sets it?

Does this part need to exist?

A part must be separate only if yes to at least one:

  1. Does it move relative to all adjacent parts in normal operation?
  2. Must it be a different material, for functional reasons?
  3. Must it be separate to allow assembly or disassembly of other parts?

Three no's → a candidate to merge with a neighbour. Cost the other side — bigger tool, stronger material — before you claim it.

Template

“[Change] takes £[x] out of every unit — £[y] a year at [volume] units — for a one-off £[z] on [the tool].” Say estimated and whose estimate it is until it is quoted.

Failure modes

Reading top to bottom — you see everything and rank nothing. Mixing a one-off into a per-unit figure. Claiming a merge without costing the bigger tool. An estimate presented as a quote. Quoting a vendor's savings figures as a forecast for your product.
