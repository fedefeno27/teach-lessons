The Options Sheet

One page: two or three costed options and a recommendation, ending in a decision rather than a debate.

Five rules

  1. The current design is an option — the reference to compare against.
  2. Options differ on a real trade-off — each wins on something.
  3. Musts first — remove anything that breaks a level-1 constraint before comparing.
  4. Better / same / worse — don't total. One number hides the trade-off.
  5. Recommend, at a level, and ask.

Failure modes

A straw man — two options there to lose. Options with no pick — reads as avoiding the risk. A pick with no options — reads as a verdict. A weighted total — small advantages outvote a must-have. Costing one option and not the others. No ask at the bottom.
