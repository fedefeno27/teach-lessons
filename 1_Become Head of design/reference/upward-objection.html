The Upward Objection

What to do when the person against you outranks the room. Rank changes the price of pushing — not the strength of your reason.

From Lesson 04 Use when A director or MD objects in review Pairs with The Justification Ladder

Three moves

1 · See them firstbefore
Fifteen minutes one-to-one. Show the decision sentence; ask “Which is your worry — cost, date, brand or risk?” No first meeting with a decision in front of an audience.
2 · Speak their unitin the room
Margin points, pounds at volume, weeks to launch, warranty exposure — whatever they answer for.
3 · Offer a stepthe ask
Ask for support, not approval. A dated test with the line drawn first, or two options and your pick. Never a stall.

If you lose

DoDon't
Say the disagreement once, at its true level. “This one's my judgement, and I'd choose the other. I'll build it your way.”Re-argue it every review, or disguise judgement as something firmer.
Commit where people can see it, and build it properly.Quietly build your own version, or sulk.
Write one line: decided, by whom, when, why, what would reopen it.Leave it unrecorded — you lose the way back.

The exception

Level 1 — law, safety, a hard process limit — is not yours to commit to. Take it to whoever owns compliance or safety, and tell the objector first that you're doing it.

Failure modes

Fighting — win the point, lose the standing. Folding — concede everything, get read as having no view. Escalating without telling them. A step used to stall. Committing on paper and undermining in practice.
