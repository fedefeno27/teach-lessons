# Starting position: Senior IC with all four leadership gaps open

Federico is a Senior Industrial Designer with no direct reports, open to either an internal
promotion or an external move into a Head of Design role. Asked to name his gap, he selected
every option offered — commercial fluency, leading people, strategy and vision, executive
influence — and then added one of his own: **"storytelling the decision."**

## Why this steers future sessions

Selecting all four means the gaps are not yet differentiated to him; he cannot rank them, which
is itself information. Do not ask him to prioritise between them again until he has evidence
from live attempts. Sequence by what is practisable *without permission* from his current seat,
not by what is most important in the abstract — he cannot practise managing people he does not
have, but he can practise arguing in money tomorrow.

The volunteered fifth gap is the highest-signal item on the list. Unprompted disclosure beats
selection from a menu, so weight "storytelling the decision" above the four he was offered.
Lesson 0001 was built at the intersection of that gap and commercial fluency, since the two
collapse into a single skill: stating a design decision as a business argument.

## Implications

- **Not yet known:** whether he sits in commercial/roadmap meetings, whether he has visibility
  of product COGS, and whether he informally mentors anyone. All three are logged as open
  questions in [[NOTES.md]]. The COGS answer in particular changes what can be taught next.
- **People leadership is currently unpractisable** in his seat. Before teaching it, find him a
  proxy: mentoring, running critique, or leading a contractor/junior on a project.
- No evidence of learning has been observed yet. This record captures a *starting position*
  only — nothing here should be treated as a demonstrated skill.
