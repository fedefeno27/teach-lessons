Federico confirmed on 25 August 2026 that **X7PP is the Invesco STOXX Europe 600 Optimised
Banks UCITS ETF** — a 49-holding European *bank sector* fund, not the broad Europe 600 index.
That single fact reorganises the whole diagnosis and sets the agenda for Arc B.

## The look-through finding

Sector exposure is not what a fund is called; it is the weighted sum of what each fund holds.
Computed across both sleeves on the £15,939.49 combined portfolio:

| Fund | Portfolio weight | Financials within fund | Contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| X7PP · Europe 600 Banks | 18.26% | 100% | 18.26% |
| XGSD · Global Select Dividend 100 | 17.36% | ~30% *(estimate — verify)* | ~5.21% |
| PAXG · MSCI Pacific ex Japan | 7.61% | 49.4% | 3.76% |
| VUAG · S&P 500 | 19.37% | 12.3% | 2.38% |
| IWFM · World Momentum | 4.46% | 19.8% | 0.88% |
| UC64 · MSCI UK | 1.25% | ~20% *(estimate)* | ~0.25% |
| NATP, IESU, SGLN, COMX, CSH2 | 31.69% | ~0% | 0% |
| **Total** | | | **≈ 30.7% of portfolio** |

Equity is 77.8% of the portfolio, so financials are **≈ 40% of the equity sleeve**, against
**17.18%** in the MSCI ACWI (July 2026). That is roughly a **2.3× overweight**, and about
sixty percent of it sits in one 49-stock fund. Counting banks specifically rather than all
financials, the overweight is larger still — Australian banks arrive via PAXG, and more banks
arrive via the dividend screen.

**Nobody decided this.** Federico decided to overweight Europe. The portfolio implements that
as a concentrated bet on European and Australian bank balance sheets. Those are different
propositions, and the gap between them is the single most valuable thing learned so far.

## The second structural finding

Splitting equity by construction method rather than by geography:

- **Broad market-cap funds:** VUAG, PAXG, UC64 → 28.2% of portfolio, **36% of equity**
- **Sector, thematic, factor and screen funds:** X7PP, XGSD, NATP, IWFM, IESU → 49.6% of
  portfolio, **64% of equity**

Nearly two-thirds of the equity is in active bets wearing index clothing. Every one may be
defensible individually; collectively they were never summed.

## Also established

- **X7PP is swap-based (synthetic)**, Irish-domiciled, TER 0.20%, accumulating. Synthetic
  replication is legitimate and sometimes advantageous, but holding 18% of a portfolio in a
  swap structure should be a decision, not a surprise. Confirms Lesson 1's point that the
  ticker tells you nothing.
- **ISA-only is a genuine advantage.** No CGT, no dividend tax, no ERI, no reporting-fund
  concerns. The cost of restructuring is spreads plus dealing fees. The usual reason to
  tolerate a bad allocation — an unrealised gain you cannot afford to crystallise — does not
  apply here.

## Implications for teaching

- Lesson 2 is the **look-through X-ray**, built on these actual numbers, with the financials
  figure as the payoff. Written and delivered 25 Aug 2026.
- Arc B continues into overlap and the core/satellite question: a portfolio can hold every one
  of these funds and still have a core, if a core is added and the satellites are capped.
- Arc C needs the contributions question resolved before rebalancing policy can be taught.
- **Do not tell Federico to sell anything.** He confirmed the Europe tilt is deliberate; the
  job is to make each bet explicit and sized, then let him choose. Teach the tool, not the trade.
