# Starting portfolio: two sleeves, eleven funds, no core

Federico shared screenshots of two live portfolios on 25 August 2026 — labelled "DiY" and
"experiment". This record fixes the starting state so future lessons can be built against
real holdings rather than a hypothetical, and so drift can be measured from a known baseline.

The headline: **target weights already exist** (the platform shows an actual/target pair for
every line), which means the earlier self-assessment of "no system" was too harsh. There is a
system. It is unimplemented, unexplained, and internally inconsistent — which is a different
and much more tractable problem than having no system at all.

## What this tells future sessions

1. **There is no core.** Neither sleeve contains a global all-cap or developed-world fund.
   The portfolio is built entirely from satellites — a region, a dividend screen, a country,
   a theme, a factor, a sector, two real-asset lines — with nothing at the centre. This is the
   single most important structural fact about it and it should drive Lesson 2.

2. **Geography is inverted relative to global market cap.** Europe plus the UK is the largest
   equity block, while US exposure sits around a quarter of the portfolio against roughly
   two-thirds of world market capitalisation. That may be a deliberate home-bias decision, or
   it may be an accident of buying interesting things. Which of the two it is has not yet been
   established, and it matters more than any fund choice.

3. **Targets exist but were never built out.** UC64 sits at 1.8% against a 15% target; IESU at
   1.9% against 10%. These are not drifted positions — they were never funded. Two sub-£200
   holdings in a £16k portfolio cannot influence returns and do add dealing cost and
   attention cost.

4. **Documented duplication.** IESU (S&P 500 Energy) is a subset of VUAG (S&P 500) — every
   IESU holding is already owned inside VUAG. IWFM is a developed-world momentum screen and so
   is majority-US, overlapping VUAG again. UC64 (UK) overlaps X7PP (Europe 600) if X7PP turns
   out to be the broad index. Overlap is the cost nobody prices.

5. **Two large concentrated bets are sitting on big gains.** NATP (defence) is +39% and XGSD
   is +47%. Both are thematic or screen-based rather than broad-market. Positions that have
   run hard are exactly where recency bias does its work, and where a rebalancing policy would
   be doing its job — if one existed.

6. **≈19% in gold and commodities** is a substantial real-asset allocation for an equity-growth
   mandate over 10–20 years. It needs an articulated reason. Currently there is none on record.

7. **Structure question flagged, not answered:** the X7PP name is truncated in the screenshot as
   "Invesco STOXX Europe 600 Optimised…", and Invesco's Optimised range includes *sector*
   funds as well as the broad index. Until the full name is confirmed, the largest holding in
   the DiY sleeve is of unknown breadth. Also unconfirmed: SGLN is an ETC (a debt security),
   not a UCITS fund, and COMX and CSH2 may share that structure — a real distinction, since
   UCITS diversification and counterparty protections do not apply to ETCs.

## Implications

- Lesson 2 must be a **portfolio X-ray on these actual holdings**: look-through exposure,
  overlap, and the gap between target and actual — not a generic treatment of weighting.
- Arc C (policy, rebalancing bands, contribution routing) now has an obvious anchor: the
  target weights already on screen. The work is to justify them, or replace them, and then
  write the rule that maintains them.
- The two-sleeve split ("DiY" vs "experiment") needs a stated purpose. If "experiment" is a
  deliberate satellite sandbox with a size cap, that is a legitimate structure worth
  formalising. If it is just a second pile, merging the view is the first step.
